\begin{reviewercomment}{Comment 3.2}
Also, a more rigorous complexity and scalability analysis is needed for the
Improved Dung Beetle Optimization algorithm under cooperative deflection
environments where communication delays influence distributed consensus
efficiency.
\end{reviewercomment}

\rsp We sincerely thank the reviewer for this important suggestion. In
response, we conducted a dedicated assignment-layer study that separates the
computational cost of IDBO from the communication cost of its distributed
Top-$L_{\max}$ consensus. No downstream guidance or interception simulation is
included in this study. The nominal problem contains 20 interceptors and 8
targets, consistent with the revised manuscript. The scalability sweep preserves
this ratio and considers $10\!\times\!4$, $20\!\times\!8$,
$40\!\times\!16$, $80\!\times\!32$, and $160\!\times\!64$ assignments.

For the current full-swarm reference implementation, each candidate contains an
$M\times N$ preference matrix, and the pairwise competition term evaluates an
$M\times M$ matrix for each target. Its dominant computational upper bound is
therefore $O(TPNM^2)$, whereas a distributed implementation restricted to a
bounded neighbor set of degree $\Delta_i$ has a per-interceptor local-search
cost of $O(TPN\Delta_i)$. Merging the Top-$L_{\max}$ records from the
neighboring interceptors requires
$O\!\left(N\Delta_iL_{\max}\log(\Delta_iL_{\max})\right)$ operations and
$O(N\Delta_iL_{\max})$ communicated records per exchange. For a static
assignment snapshot, parallel winner-record propagation requires
$O(D(d+1))$ exchanges, where $D$ is the graph diameter and $d$ is the bounded
additional delay measured in exchange periods; $O(ND(d+1))$ remains a
conservative CBBA bundle-construction bound.

The measured IDBO runtime grew approximately linearly with the local population
size (log--log slope 0.84). When the interceptor and target numbers were enlarged
together at the nominal ratio, the fitted exponent was 1.45. At a fixed
optimization budget, the CPU times were $0.048$ s for the nominal
$20\!\times\!8$ problem and $1.948$ s for $160\!\times\!64$. We therefore no
longer describe joint problem-size growth as unconditionally linear.

We next generated 20 independent three-dimensional $20\!\times\!8$ assignment
snapshots and explicitly delayed each neighbor-to-neighbor winner-record
message. A data-link exchange period of 50 ms was used, and the additional
per-hop delay was varied over $0$, $50$, $100$, $200$, and $400$ ms. The mean
times to the verified global Top-$L_{\max}$ fixed point were, respectively,
$0.53\pm0.08$, $0.96\pm0.17$, $1.39\pm0.25$,
$2.25\pm0.42$, and $3.97\pm0.76$ s. Every one of the 100 runs reached the
same checked fixed point. At a fixed 100-ms delay, increasing the communication
diameter from $D=1,3,4,5,$ to $10$ increased the consensus time from
$0.25,0.55,0.70,0.85,$ to $1.60$ s, confirming the predicted linear dependence
on the propagation diameter.

Finally, to expose the limitation of the static result, we constructed eight
independent cooperative-deflection sequences, each containing 30 assignment
snapshots. The targets executed coordinated alternating lateral deflection, and
the allocation was refreshed every 2 s. For additional delays of
$0,50,100,200,$ and $400$ ms, the mean winner-set agreement was
$0.938,0.876,0.814,0.697,$ and $0.545$, while the fractions of changed
allocations that recovered before the next update were
$100\%,100\%,100\%,41.2\%,$ and $0.9\%$, respectively. Thus, bounded delay
preserves the eventual fixed point for a static snapshot, but excessive delay
relative to the reallocation period prevents the distributed replicas from
tracking a rapidly changing assignment. The revised discussion now states the
required operating condition explicitly: the consensus time should remain
shorter than the reallocation interval. The full protocol, raw CSV data,
reproducible code, tables, and vector figures are provided with this response.
