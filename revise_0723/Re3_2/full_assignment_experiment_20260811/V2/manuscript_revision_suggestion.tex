\hladd{Let $M$, $N$, $P$, $T$, $\Delta_i$, and $L_{\max}$ denote the
numbers of interceptors and targets, the IDBO population size, the local-search
iterations, the communication degree of interceptor $i$, and the per-target
winner capacity, respectively. In the full-swarm reference implementation, the
pairwise competition term requires $O(NM^2)$ operations per candidate, giving
$O(TPNM^2)$ as the dominant computational upper bound. In a distributed
implementation restricted to the neighbor set $\mathcal N_i$, the corresponding
per-interceptor term reduces to $O(TPN\Delta_i)$. Each consensus exchange merges
at most $L_{\max}$ records per target and neighbor, requiring
$O\!\left(N\Delta_iL_{\max}\log(\Delta_iL_{\max})\right)$ operations and
$O(N\Delta_iL_{\max})$ communicated records.}

\hladd{For a connected communication graph of diameter $D$, a static set of
winner records propagates in $O(D(d+1))$ exchanges when the bounded additional
single-hop delay is $d$ exchange periods; $O(ND(d+1))$ is a conservative bound
when the CBBA bundle is constructed sequentially over the targets. In the
assignment-only experiments, the 20-interceptor/8-target case is used as the
nominal setting. All static delayed runs reached the same verified
Top-$L_{\max}$ fixed point, while the consensus time increased approximately
linearly with delay and graph diameter. Under coordinated target deflection and
a 2-s reallocation interval, delays up to 100 ms recovered before the next
update in all tested sequences, whereas 200--400 ms delays caused substantial
tracking degradation. Hence, static convergence additionally requires the
online consensus time to remain shorter than the reallocation interval in a
time-varying engagement.}
