## Reviewer comment

> Then, it would be helpful to add an ablation investigation demonstrating the independent contribution of the trust-aware mechanism, GRU temporal encoder, and attention-residual backbone within ART-MAPPO architecture.

## Response

Thank you for this constructive recommendation. We added a component-wise ablation in both paper scenarios. Four variants were trained separately in each scenario: the complete ART-MAPPO, ART-MAPPO without trust-aware exploration, without the GRU encoder, and without the attention-residual backbone. Each of the eight runs used the same seed, optimizer, reward, dynamics, 585-update/599,040-step budget, and scenario-specific initial-state distribution. The attention-residual control was capacity matched (732.8k versus 733.2k actor parameters), so its comparison is not explained by parameter count. Checkpoints were selected on 20 validation episodes whose seeds were disjoint from the formal tests. We then froze the selected policies and ran 100 paired Monte Carlo episodes per variant and scenario (800 episodes total); evaluation performed no backpropagation or optimizer updates.

The added training curves establish different, complementary contributions. First, removing trust caused substantially faster exploration collapse: the final policy entropy changed from 1.159 to 0.517 in Case 1 and from 1.301 to 0.756 in Case 2, whereas the trust-enabled policy retained a guided-action fraction of 0.535/0.523. This directly supports the stated training-side role of the trust-aware mechanism: it modulates the balance between the analytical guidance prior and learned exploration. Second, removing the GRU increased final-window critic loss from 0.0583 to 0.3095 in Case 1 (5.31 times) and from 0.0348 to 0.2330 in Case 2 (6.69 times), with substantially larger loss variance. Thus, the clearest independent contribution of the GRU is stable temporal value estimation. Third, the capacity-matched no-attention-residual control nearly overlaps the complete model in return AUC and final critic loss under the nominal scenarios, but it retains lower final policy entropy in Case 2 (1.192 versus 1.301) and incurs a slightly larger terminal normal load in Case 1 (mean 0.0340 g versus 0.0316 g). We therefore describe this backbone as providing a modest representation and safety margin under these nominal conditions, rather than claiming a universal gain unsupported by the data.

All eight frozen-policy cells achieved 100/100 target coverage, cooperative success, and strict mission success; the Wilson 95% interval is 96.3%--100%. Exact paired McNemar tests therefore give p=1. This success-rate ceiling is itself informative: the analytical guidance layer and interception redundancy allow all four architectures to complete the nominal tasks, so binary success cannot separate the modules. We consequently added the four continuous metrics used in the simulation section. In Case 1, the full model reduced engagement duration by 0.419 s relative to no trust, 0.108 s relative to no GRU, and 0.032 s relative to no attention-residual (paired 95% bootstrap intervals exclude zero). It also reduced terminal normal load relative to no GRU and no attention-residual by approximately 0.00238 g. These improvements coexist with a 0.0067--0.0111 s increase in the full model's group timing error, which remains far below the 0.5-s cooperative threshold. In Case 2, the continuous distributions largely overlap; the only practically visible paired effect is a 0.0278 s shorter duration than no GRU, while other differences are small or their confidence intervals include zero.

Accordingly, the revision does not use a forced ranking in which the full model must win every nominal metric. Instead, it provides the component-level evidence requested by the reviewer: trust-aware exploration preserves controlled exploration, the GRU stabilizes temporal critic learning, and the attention-residual backbone offers a capacity-controlled representation/safety margin. The common 100% nominal success additionally documents architectural redundancy, while the paired continuous metrics define the magnitude and limits of each module's operational effect. We added the training-return, critic-loss, and policy-entropy comparisons, the 100-trial success summary, and boxplots of E_co-time, E_n, E_miss, and E_t to the revised manuscript, together with an explicit discussion of this ceiling effect and the one-training-seed limitation.
